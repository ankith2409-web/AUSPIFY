01 / CHALLENGE

People in distress need certainty about whether a request reached anyone.

Use plain-language states and a visible call alternative at each critical point.

Patient delivery-state study and a three-role local handoff demo. Real dispatch requires institutional integration.

01 / DISCOVERY

Start with the service that already exists.

India’s 112 Emergency Response Support System already accepts requests across channels and supports dispatch. The 112 India app also describes status tracking. A new concept cannot honestly claim to invent those capabilities. The useful design question is how to make handoff states easier to understand and recover from.

What published user studies add

Rochat and colleagues tested InfoKids with 17 parents. They found 14 usability problems, seven rated major or catastrophic, despite positive overall usability ratings. That result is a reason to observe individual tasks and recovery paths rather than infer ease of use from a polished screen. This study concerns pediatric emergency-department care in Geneva; it does not validate this Bengaluru coordination concept.

Romano and colleagues evaluated a different emergency-notification app using a simulated accident and expert interviews. It provides a precedent for contextual evaluation, not evidence that this prototype improves emergency response.

Original studies: Rochat et al., 2022 · Romano et al., 2016. Full evidence and assumption register.

P1 / HYPOTHESIS

A bystander on a phone

Goal: share an understandable pickup point and know who accepted. Possible difficulty: mistaking a sent request for an assigned ambulance. Design response: distinct waiting and accepted states with a direct-call option. Validate the wording with local participants.

P2 / HYPOTHESIS

A hospital coordinator

Goal: review the pickup and need before assigning a sample vehicle. Possible difficulty: losing a partly entered identifier while changing views. Design response: retain the draft and gate dispatch after acceptance. Actual triage and workload are unresearched.

P3 / HYPOTHESIS

A traffic coordinator

Goal: understand and acknowledge a proposed route alert. Possible difficulty: assuming arrival proves acknowledgement. Design response: retain the separate acknowledgement event. This proposed role needs validation with authorized staff.

These are provisional personas, not interview participants. Secondary research is documented; firsthand research for this product has not been conducted.

02 / FRAMING

Make each handoff understandable.

A

Who this is for

A patient or bystander on a phone, possibly stressed, outdoors, or on an unreliable connection. This is a design assumption, not a researched persona from interviews.

B

What they need

A short request, confidence that it was delivered, the identity of whoever accepted it, and an unambiguous alternative when it fails or no provider responds.

C

What the interface must say

“Not sent,” “sent and waiting,” and “accepted” are separate states. No estimated arrival appears before assignment. Sample hospital details are marked as simulation.

Patient journey

  1. LocateConfirm the pickup point.
  2. DescribeChoose a category and add contact details.
  3. ReviewCheck before sending.
  4. WaitSee delivery and acceptance status.
  5. ActTrack the proposed handoff or call 112.

Design decision: make the next step clear while waiting

Three-role service journey

  1. PatientCreate a sample request.
  2. HospitalReview, accept, and assign a vehicle.
  3. TrafficAcknowledge the route alert.
  4. PatientRead the current handoff.
  5. CompleteRetain the completed details.

03 / SOLUTION

Explore delivery and recovery states.

This focused patient study explores review, simulated delivery, failed send, and no response. The separate working demo models the three roles with shared browser-local state. Use sample details in both; neither connects to emergency services.

    04 / DESIGN SYSTEM

    Calm structure. Urgent action.

    The visual system reserves red for the main request action. Navy carries readable text; teal marks information and selection. Status uses words as well as color, so an error is never communicated by a red dot alone.

    05 / EVALUATION

    What the review found.

    Internal review and revisions

    • Completed requests now retain pickup, hospital, vehicle, and handoff history.
    • Changing roles preserves an unfinished request or vehicle draft.
    • Arrival and traffic acknowledgement remain separate events.
    • Review, failed send, sent, accepted, and no response have distinct screens.

    Evidence still needed

    • Observed sessions with local users; provisional personas remain unvalidated.
    • Hospital and traffic-staff research to establish a viable service workflow.
    • Screen reader, physical-device, and unreliable-network evaluation.
    • Institutional integration before any live emergency use.

    Outcome: a documented design concept and interactive prototypes, with internal checks recorded in the evaluation checklist. No participant success rate or emergency response improvement is claimed. The next study protocol is ready; no participant sessions have been completed.
