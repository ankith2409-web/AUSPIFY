01 / CHALLENGE

People in distress need certainty about whether a request reached anyone.

02 / DESIGN RESPONSE

Use plain-language states and a visible call alternative at each critical point.

03 / SCOPE

Patient-facing concept. Dispatch, clinical triage, and police coordination require institutional integration.

01 / DISCOVERY

Start with the service that already exists.

India’s 112 Emergency Response Support System already accepts requests across channels and supports dispatch. The 112 India app also describes status tracking. A new concept cannot honestly claim to invent those capabilities. The useful design question is how to make handoff states easier to understand and recover from.

02 / FRAMING

Design for uncertainty, not an imaginary perfect network.

A

Who this is for

A patient or bystander on a phone, possibly stressed, outdoors, or on an unreliable connection. This is a design assumption, not a researched persona from interviews.

B

What they need

A short request, confidence that it was delivered, the identity of whoever accepted it, and an unambiguous alternative when it fails or no provider responds.

C

What the interface must say

“Not sent,” “sent and waiting,” and “accepted” are separate states. No estimated arrival appears before assignment. Sample hospital details are marked as simulation.

Patient journey

  1. LocateConfirm the pickup point.
  2. DescribeChoose a category and add contact details.
  3. ReviewCheck before sending.
  4. WaitSee delivery and acceptance status.
  5. ActTrack the proposed handoff or call 112.

The low fidelity exploration is in Task 1’s editable wireframes ↗. The interactive screens below refine its status and failure branches.

    04 / DESIGN SYSTEM

    Calm structure. Urgent action.

    The visual system reserves red for the main request action. Navy carries readable text; teal marks information and selection. Status uses words as well as color, so an error is never communicated by a red dot alone.

    05 / EVALUATION

    What was checked—and what remains unproven.

    Verified in this prototype

    • All visible controls have a defined response.
    • Keyboard focus, form labels, and status text are present.
    • Review, delivered, accepted, failed send, and no-response states are distinct.
    • The layout adapts to narrow screens and desktop.

    Needed before a real product

    • Interviews and observed usability sessions with diverse users.
    • Partnerships, contracts, and verified dispatch integration.
    • Privacy, security, consent, and data retention review.
    • Device testing, screen reader testing, and unreliable-network trials.

    Outcome: a reviewable concept and working UI prototype. No user research findings, clinical outcomes, or real emergency response improvements are claimed.
