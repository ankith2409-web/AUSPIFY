# Task 5 — participant usability study

**Status:** not conducted. Participants: **0**. The protocol and empty CSV are preparation, not results. Internal agent/browser QA must not be entered as a participant session.

## Aim and setup

Observe whether customers can find an object, understand total cost, edit a bag, recover from checkout errors, and recognize that the confirmation is a simulation. Plan an initial round with five adults who shop online, including people using a phone and keyboard navigation. Five is a proposed formative sample, not a guarantee of finding every issue. Obtain consent; use anonymous IDs P01–P05 and invented contact/address details. Do not record unnecessary personal information.

Moderator introduction: “We are testing the design, not you. Please think aloud. Use the sample details provided. There is no real purchase.” Do not explain the controls before a task. Record any help given. Ask permission before recording; notes alone are sufficient.

| ID | Neutral task prompt | Observation and success definition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| U1 | Find the least expensive decor object and learn what it is made of. | Participant independently finds Glass Vase, $96, recycled glass |
| U2 | Put two of those objects in your bag. What is the total including shipping? | Quantity 2; subtotal $192, shipping $18, total $210; notes whether shipping is understood |
| U3 | You only need one now. Make that change, then remove it entirely. | Quantity decreases; empty bag and recovery understood |
| U4 | Add an object and try checkout. Correct any missing details. Before finishing, return to the bag and then continue again. | Participant recovers from validation and discovers retained entries |
| U5 | Finish the sample checkout. Explain what happens next. | Participant understands no actual order, payment or delivery exists |

## Record and analyze

For each task record independent success, assisted success, failure/abandonment, time in seconds, errors, assistance, observed hesitation, and a direct quote only when actually spoken. After each task ask ease from 1 (very difficult) to 7 (very easy). Ask “What was unclear?” after the final task. Leave unavailable measures blank; never infer or manufacture them.

Use participant-results.csv. Summarize independent success as successful independent attempts / observed attempts, with raw counts. Separate incomplete tasks. A small formative study supports design iteration, not population-level conversion claims.

Severity: blocker = cannot complete; major = completes only with help or makes a consequential misunderstanding; minor = reversible hesitation. Revise blockers and major problems, then repeat affected tasks with fresh participants where feasible. Proposed acceptance gate: no blockers in the observed sample, no mistaken belief that a real purchase occurred, and all participants can recover from required-field errors without help. These are planned targets; no outcome is claimed.
